## Dan Shipper
*Dan Shipper (Co-founder, Every)*

> "AI doesn't replace PMs. It makes small PM teams as powerful as large ones."

**Insight:** The productivity multiplier from AI isn't about headcount reduction — it's about capability expansion. A small, AI-augmented team can now execute at the scale and speed previously requiring a much larger organization. The constraint shifts from "do we have enough people" to "do we have the right strategy and distribution."

**Tactical advice:**
- Focus AI leverage on execution velocity, not headcount reduction
- Invest the time saved into faster user feedback loops and more experiments
- Distribution becomes the differentiator when AI equalizes execution capacity

---

> "Ship fast, learn from users. AI makes small teams powerful. Distribution beats perfect product."

**Insight:** In 5 AI-native products generating 7-figure revenue with a small team, the consistent pattern was rapid shipping over perfect engineering. The AI handled enough of the execution that the bottleneck became market learning, not production capacity. A good-enough product that ships beats a perfect product that doesn't.

**Tactical advice:**
- Set a maximum time-to-first-user constraint (days, not weeks)
- Build feedback loops before you build polish
- Invest in distribution channels before you optimize the product

## Brandon Chu
*Brandon Chu (VP Product, Shopify)*

> "Build for the AI you'll have in 6 months, not the AI you have today."

**Insight:** Product architecture decisions have longer half-lives than model capabilities. Building your product around today's model limitations creates technical debt that is paid when the model improves and your architecture fights it. The right abstraction layer treats the model as a swappable dependency, not a fixed component.

**Tactical advice:**
- Treat model selection as a configuration parameter, not an architectural decision
- Design prompts and pipelines for portability across model providers
- Review your product's "designed around current AI limits" decisions quarterly — many will be outdated
