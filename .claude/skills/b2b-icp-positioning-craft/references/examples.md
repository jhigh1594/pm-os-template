# Examples

Use these examples as pattern guides, not as copy-paste answers.

## Example 1: Narrowing The First Wedge

### Weak ICP
- Mid-market B2B SaaS companies
- VP of Engineering
- Need better planning

Why it is weak:
- too broad
- unclear workflow
- unclear trigger
- unclear champion

### Better ICP
- Series B to D B2B SaaS companies
- VP Engineering or Head of Platform
- teams coordinating cross-team launches with Jira already in place
- recurring pain from missed dependencies across 4 or more teams
- active trigger: recent launch slip or executive pressure on predictability

Why it is better:
- clear company context
- clearer champion
- explicit workflow
- explicit trigger
- easier to qualify

## Example 2: Separating Champion From Stakeholders

### Bad framing
- Buyer = engineering, product, finance, and operations leaders

Why it fails:
- too many people bundled into one ICP
- no clear shortlist owner
- value story becomes vague

### Better framing
- Champion: Head of Product Operations
- Economic buyer: VP Product
- Key stakeholder: Engineering Director
- Blocker: PMO lead worried about change management

Why it is better:
- the champion is explicit
- objections are easier to map
- positioning can focus on the first person who must care

## Example 3: Mapping Real Alternatives

### Bad alternative set
- Competitor A
- Competitor B
- Competitor C

Why it fails:
- ignores the status quo
- ignores internal workarounds
- assumes buyers already think in category terms

### Better alternative set
- Status quo: spreadsheets, meetings, and manual status collection
- Internal workaround: analyst-maintained planning workbook
- Direct competitor: point solution for roadmapping
- Adjacent tool: Jira Align already under evaluation

Why it is better:
- matches real buyer choices
- helps explain why no-decision happens
- surfaces where differentiation must matter

## Example 4: Running The "So What?" Ladder

### Capability
- bi-directional sync with Jira

### Weak value statement
- reduces manual work

Why it is weak:
- too generic
- many tools can claim it

### Better ladder
- Capability: bi-directional sync with Jira
- So what: planning data stays aligned with execution data
- So what: teams stop reconciling conflicting status in meetings
- Buyer-valued outcome: improve planning credibility and reduce surprise before executive reviews

Why it is better:
- lands in buyer language
- ties to a visible business outcome
- stays specific enough to differentiate

## Example 5: Separating Value From Objections

### Mistake
- Easy deployment is our main value

Why it is often wrong:
- many buyers care about deployment only after they believe the product is worth buying

### Better split
- Core value: reduce dependency-driven delivery surprises
- Likely objection: deployment effort and change management
- Follow-up proof needed: implementation path, time-to-value, customer examples

## Example 6: Signal Quality

### Noisy signal
- 8 investor intro calls said the idea sounds strong

### Stronger signal
- outbound reply rate is 3x higher in logistics companies with distributed operations teams
- 4 of 5 live deals in that segment cited the same dependency problem
- two buyers asked to start in the current quarter

Use the stronger signal to refine the ICP, not the friendlier one.
