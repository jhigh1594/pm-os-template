# Positioning

## When This Skill Activates

Use this skill when any of the following apply:

- position this, define ICP, refine ICP, target customer, narrow segment, champion profile
- market category, differentiation, value proposition, positioning statement
- April Dunford, Obviously Awesome, ICP brief, customer signal analysis
- buyer alternatives, connect to positioning, qualify messaging, discovery messaging
- value narrative, competitor positioning, how do we position this, draft an ICP brief
- turn discovery notes into messaging, what makes us different

## Core Idea

Treat ICP as a focus decision, not a description exercise.

Positioning is the external expression of that focus.

Favor the smallest repeatable segment where:
- the pain is real
- the buyer or champion cares now
- the current product wins for a differentiated reason
- the signal comes from behavior, not polite interest

**Positioning without ICP is untethered. Never skip Phase 1.**

## Three-Phase Workflow

### Phase 1: ICP Discovery & Validation

**Focus:** Who is the buyer, what's their trigger, what do they compare against?

#### Step 1: Form ICP Hypothesis

Define 3–5 attributes. Favor:
- Company size
- Role / champion
- Workflow context
- Tech environment
- Trigger event (what happened that made them look?)

Avoid broad firmographic-only ICPs. Avoid defining ICP by title alone.

#### Step 2: Identify the Champion

Separate the champion from stakeholders, influencers, and blockers. Determine:
- Who feels the pain first?
- Who makes the shortlist?
- Who can carry the deal internally?
- Who blocks?

#### Step 3: Map the Alternatives

Always include the status quo — "what would the buyer do if this product didn't exist?"

Include:
- Direct competitors
- Internal workarounds
- Adjacent tools

Never say "we have no competitors." Customers hear: "there's no demand for what you do."

#### Step 4: Assess Signal Quality

Prefer observed behavior over stated preference.
Prefer outbound and clean-market signal over warm intros or friendly conversations.

Signal rubric:
- **Behavior signal** (highest) — customer changed workflow, paid, expanded
- **Stated + consistent** — customer articulates pain and returns unprompted
- **Warm intro** (discount heavily) — polite interest from a friendly source
- **Single mention** (noise) — anecdote, not pattern

#### Step 5: Run the "So What?" Ladder

Start from features or capabilities. Ask "so what?" until value lands in buyer-language business terms. Stop before the value becomes generic and interchangeable.

Example:
- Feature: "real-time data sync" → so what?
- Outcome: "no manual exports" → so what?
- Impact: "ops team closes the books 2 days faster" ← stop here

#### Step 6: Business Model Constraint Check

After forming ICP hypothesis, check four fit signals before producing positioning assets. These are validation questions, not blockers — but any "Flag" must be surfaced before writing positioning.

**Revenue Model Fit**
Does this segment's buying behavior align with current pricing?
Signal: Has this segment closed through the same motion as other wins?

**CS Capacity Implication**
Does serving this ICP at scale create CS overhead not anticipated?
Signal: Support or onboarding signals in the workspace about this segment?

**Sales Motion Fit**
Can the current sales motion reach this ICP?
Signal: Examples of this segment in the customer base — and how were they acquired?

**Competitive Defensibility**
If we position for this ICP, which competitors can follow and which can't?
Signal: What do battlecards say about how competitors serve this segment?

```
## Business Model Fit Signals
- Revenue model: [Go/Watch/Flag] — [one line]
- CS capacity: [Go/Watch/Flag] — [one line]
- Sales motion: [Go/Watch/Flag] — [one line]
- Competitive defensibility: [Go/Watch/Flag] — [one line]
```

Any "Flag": note it prominently and recommend resolving before investing in full positioning assets.

#### Phase 1 Output

- ICP hypothesis (3–5 attributes)
- NOT-for segment (who to explicitly exclude)
- Champion deep-dive (pain holder, deal carrier, blocker)
- Alternative map with signal quality
- Differentiated value themes (in buyer language, from the "so what?" ladder)
- Business model fit check (Go/Watch/Flag per dimension)

If context is incomplete, still provide:
- A provisional hypothesis
- The biggest uncertainty
- The next 3 questions or data points needed

### Phase 2: April Dunford Positioning Framework

**Focus:** Convert Phase 1 signal into a defensible market position.

Apply the five components in order. Do not skip or reorder.

**1. WHO — Target Customer**
Lock the ICP from Phase 1. This is not a description — it's a commitment. "All types of companies" is not an answer.

**2. WHAT — Market Category**
Existing category or new? Sets customer mental model and expectations.
- Existing category: faster on-ramp, harder to differentiate
- New category: full control of framing, expensive to educate

**3. WHAT — Alternatives**
Use the alternative map from Phase 1. Never a blank here.

**4. UNIQUE VALUE**
Express Phase 1 value themes in Dunford framework terms: different AND why it matters.
- "Differentiation" without "why it matters" is just a feature claim.

**5. PROOF**
Pull signal evidence and customer behavior from Phase 1 as validation. Metrics, stories, behavior change. No proof = no positioning — it's just a hope.

#### Positioning Canvas

```markdown
# Positioning: [Product]

## 1. Best For (Target Customer)
**Ideal customer:**
- Industry: [specific]
- Role / Champion: [specific]
- Trigger: [what happened that made them look?]
- Problem: [specific pain]
- **NOT for:** [who should avoid]

## 2. Market Category
**It's a:** [category name]
**Why this category:** [how customers will frame expectations]

## 3. Alternatives
**Customers currently use:**
- [Alternative 1 — including status quo]
- [Alternative 2]
- [Alternative 3]

**Why they're insufficient:**
- [Reason tied to buyer outcome, not feature]

## 4. Unique Value
**We're different because:**
- [Differentiator 1]
- [Differentiator 2]

**Why that matters to customers (buyer language):**
- [Outcome 1]
- [Outcome 2]

## 5. Proof Points
- [Metric / behavior change / customer story]
- [Metric / behavior change / customer story]

## One-Sentence Positioning Statement
"For [target customer] who [need/problem], [product] is a [category] that [unique value]. Unlike [alternatives], we [key differentiator]."
```

### Phase 3: Messaging & GTM Assets

**Focus:** Translate positioning into language sales and marketing can use.

#### Qualification Message
Attributes that identify an in-ICP prospect. Designed for SDR or AE use in outbound.
- ICP firmographic attributes
- Champion signal triggers (what tells you this person has the job-to-be-done?)
- Anti-ICP flags (who to disqualify fast)

#### Discovery Message
Opens the conversation around the champion's struggling moment.
- Lead with the trigger, not the product
- Reference the alternative they're likely using today
- Ask about outcomes, not features

#### Value Narrative
3–5 sentences in buyer language. Rules:
- Lead with outcome, not feature
- Reference the alternative for contrast (makes differentiation concrete)
- Include one proof point (behavior or metric)
- No internal jargon — every word must pass the "would a buyer say this?" test

#### Validation Triggers
List the next discovery questions or evidence that would either strengthen or challenge this positioning.
- What would confirm the ICP hypothesis?
- What would invalidate the differentiator?
- What proof point would most change buyer confidence?

## Guardrails

**Do:**
- Always start with ICP as a focus decision, not a description exercise — choose the smallest repeatable segment
- Always include status quo in the alternative map
- Always distinguish stated preference (discount) from behavior signal (prefer)
- Always run "so what?" ladder to buyer language before writing copy
- Always check business model fit before investing in full positioning deck
- Use Phase 3 validation triggers as a follow-up list, not just asset creation

**Do not:**
- Skip ICP work and jump to April Dunford (positioning without ICP is untethered)
- Write positioning statements before the alternative map is done
- Accept "we serve all types of companies" — force the focus decision
- Use internal jargon in value themes — every word must be buyer language
- Assume ICP without behavior signal — stated preference alone is insufficient
- Define ICP by title alone
- Confuse enthusiasm with buying signal
- Trust warm-intro demand as primary evidence
- Position against future-state product claims
- Turn every feature into a value theme
- Broaden the segment before repeatable wins exist

## What Makes This Skill Different

A naive response skips Phase 1 entirely and jumps to writing a positioning statement. This produces positioning that is internally logical but externally untethered — it describes the product, not the buyer's world.

This skill forces three things most AI responses skip:
1. ICP as a focus decision with explicit NOT-for definition
2. Alternative map that always includes status quo
3. "So what?" ladder that stops at buyer-language business terms, not generic claims
