# Product Taste & Intuition

Develop product taste and intuition using frameworks from product leaders. This skill is about **building the underlying capability** of product judgment—distinct from evaluating specific artifacts.

## How to Help

When the user asks for help with product taste:

1. **Understand their current exposure** — Ask about the products they use and analyze regularly
2. **Identify gaps in their intuition** — Determine where their product judgment feels weakest
3. **Suggest deliberate practice** — Recommend specific activities to build taste over time
4. **Help them trust their gut** — Guide them on when to rely on intuition vs. data

---

## Core Principles

### Intuition is a Hypothesis Generator
> "I think intuition is like a hypothesis generator and you're constantly generating these hypotheses and others are generating hypotheses as well." — Dylan Field (Figma)

Intuition isn't about being right—it's about generating good hypotheses quickly that you can then validate. Your gut gives you a starting point; data tells you if you're right.

**How to apply**: When you have a product intuition, treat it as a hypothesis to test, not a conclusion to defend.

### Taste is the Differentiator in an AI World
> "In this cacophony, how do you stand out? You stand out by having good taste. I think taste is the most important thing." — Alex Komoroske

As AI makes production easier, taste becomes the critical differentiator that separates great products from "slop." Technical execution is commoditized; judgment is scarce.

### Taste is a Developable Skill
> "Taste, sometimes I think we think of as this inaccessible thing that, 'Oh, that person was born with taste.' I see it as a skill that it can develop." — Guillermo Rauch (Vercel)

Taste is built through "exposure hours" and deliberately analyzing the best products in the world, not innate talent. It's a craft that improves with practice.

**How to apply**: Schedule regular time to analyze excellent products. Keep a "taste journal" of observations.

### Build Taste Through Self-Observation
> "The number one advice... is it's just really about observation and it's about curiosity and can start by first observing yourself." — Julie Zhuo

Build product sense by noticing your own reactions to products, then validating those observations qualitatively and quantitatively. Your own frustration and delight are data.

**Exercise**: When you feel delighted or frustrated by a product, pause and ask: "What specifically caused this reaction? How was it designed to create this feeling?"

### Be a Voracious User of Products
> "The best cheat codes for getting better at building products is just being a voracious user of products... There's just no replacement for that." — Kayvon Beykpour (Twitter)

Building great consumer products relies on "muscle memory" developed by using many products deeply. You can't design what you haven't experienced.

**How to apply**: Use competitor products, adjacent products, and products outside your industry regularly. Notice what works and what doesn't.

### Taste Means Knowing What to Remove
Great taste isn't just about what to add—it's about knowing what to cut. The ability to simplify and focus is a core expression of product taste.

> "Perfection is achieved not when there is nothing more to add, but when there is nothing left to take away." — Antoine de Saint-Exupéry

## Building Taste: Deliberate Practice

### Exposure Hours
- Use 3-5 products outside your industry weekly
- Analyze one excellent product in depth monthly
- Keep notes on what you observe

### Pattern Recognition
- Identify recurring patterns across successful products
- Notice when products break patterns successfully
- Build a mental library of "what works"

### Self-Observation Journal
Track your own reactions:
- What delighted you today? What specifically caused it?
- What frustrated you today? What would have fixed it?
- When did you feel clever? When did you feel confused?

### Design Critique Practice
- Regularly critique designs without the designer present
- Predict what will work before you see data
- Review your predictions against actual performance

## When to Trust Intuition vs Data

| Trust Intuition When | Trust Data When |
|---------------------|-----------------|
| No data exists yet | Data is abundant and clear |
| Exploring new problem spaces | Optimizing existing features |
| Inventing solutions | Diagnosing problems |
| Speed matters more than precision | Stakes are very high |
| Creative decisions | Factual questions |

> "Data is not a tool that's going to tell you what you should build... but it can tell you if you have a problem." — Julie Zhuo

## Questions to Help Users

- "What products do you use daily that you think are exceptionally well-designed? What makes them great?"
- "When was the last time you analyzed a competitor's product in detail? What did you learn?"
- "When you use a new app, what do you notice first? What bothers you?"
- "How often do you experiment with products outside your industry or domain?"
- "When your intuition and data conflict, how do you decide what to do?"

---

## Common Mistakes to Flag

- **Treating taste as innate** — Believing some people just "have it" rather than developing it deliberately
- **Not using enough products** — Limited exposure leading to limited intuition
- **Ignoring your own reactions** — Not paying attention to what delights or frustrates you as a user
- **Over-relying on data** — Never making decisions based on judgment when data is unavailable
- **Copying without understanding** — Adopting patterns from successful products without understanding why they work
