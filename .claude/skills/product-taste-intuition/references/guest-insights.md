# Developing Product Taste - All Guest Insights

*10 guests, 11 mentions*

---

## Alex Komoroske

> "In this cacophony, how do you stand out? You stand out by having good taste. I think taste is the most important thing."

**Insight:** Taste is the most critical differentiator in an era of AI-generated 'slop.' As AI makes production easier, the ability to recognize and create quality becomes the scarce resource.

---

## Dylan Field
*(2 mentions)*

> "I think intuition is like a hypothesis generator and you're constantly generating these hypotheses and others are generating hypotheses as well."

**Insight:** Intuition is a 'hypothesis generator' and a core superpower for making product decisions — not a conclusion mechanism, but a starting point for validation.

---

> "Taste is your point of view on things and how do you develop your point of view... build your repertoire. Understand what is the greater context, what is the canon that led to this thing, and where do you disagree or agree philosophically?"

**Insight:** Taste is a point of view. You develop it by building a repertoire, studying the canon of great work in your domain, and reflecting on your philosophical agreements and disagreements with it.

**Tactical advice:**
- Build a deliberate repertoire of excellent products you've analyzed in depth
- Study the "canon" — the best examples of what has worked and why
- Form and document your own philosophical stance on product decisions

## Guillermo Rauch

> "Taste, sometimes I think we think of as this inaccessible thing that, 'Oh, that person was born with taste.' I see it as a skill that it can develop."

**Insight:** Taste is not innate — it's built through 'exposure hours' and deliberately analyzing the best products in the world.

**Tactical advice:**
- Schedule structured time to analyze excellent products (not just use them)
- Keep a taste journal of what you observe and why it works

---

## Julie Zhuo

> "The number one advice... is it's just really about observation and it's about curiosity and can start by first observing yourself."

**Insight:** Build product sense through self-observation and qualitative/quantitative validation. Your own frustration and delight are primary data sources.

**Tactical advice:**
- When you feel delighted or frustrated by a product, pause and diagnose: what specifically caused this?
- Validate your observations both qualitatively (talk to others) and quantitatively (look at data)

## Kayvon Beykpour

> "The best cheat codes for getting better at building products is just being a voracious user of products... There's just no replacement for that."

**Insight:** Building great consumer products relies on 'muscle memory' and 'taste' developed by being a voracious user of products. There is no shortcut.

## Seth Godin

> "I define good taste as knowing what other people want just before they do."

**Insight:** Good taste is anticipatory — it's about understanding human needs and desires just before they become explicit. This is what separates trend-setters from followers.

## Shreyas Doshi

> "Taste is about the ability to identify what is really good, without needing to see its results."

**Insight:** Taste is the ability to identify quality and truth without needing social proof or results validation. It's judgment that precedes evidence.

---

## Naomi Gleit

> "A PM cannot outsource their perspective or delegate their thinking through people and process."

**Insight:** Developing a first-party perspective is non-negotiable. PMs who outsource their thinking to team process lose the ability to make genuine product judgments.

---

## Tamar Yehoshua

> "You need to understand motivations in people for building products... why does somebody want to click on a button, why does somebody want to join your team?"

**Insight:** Product taste requires understanding human motivations at a deep level — the 'why' behind behavior, not just the behavior itself.
